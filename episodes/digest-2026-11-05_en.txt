And a shorter one: why C V S is splitting up.

The company will spin off its roughly nine thousand drugstores as C V S Pharmacy in the first half of twenty twenty-eight. The insurer Aetna, bought for seventy-eight billion dollars in twenty eighteen, stays with Caremark, the pharmacy-benefit manager, as C V S Health.

The company's stated logic is focus. The pressure was also outside. In May, Elizabeth Warren and Josh Hawley reintroduced a bill to bar pharmacy-benefit managers from owning pharmacies, and two states have passed laws to the same effect, which C V S is fighting in court. One idea before we finish.

In September nineteen forty-five, Friedrich Hayek, then at the London School of Economics, published an essay in the American Economic Review called The Use of Knowledge in Society. His claim was that the economic problem is using knowledge nobody holds in full, like the shipper who knows a tramp steamer is sailing half empty. A price, Hayek argued, compresses all of that into one number. His example was tin. If tin becomes scarce, users don't need to know why. The price rises, and they economize.

The circumstance was a fight. Through the nineteen thirties, Oskar Lange and other socialist economists had argued that a planning board could set prices by trial and error as well as any market. Hayek was answering them.

The complication arrived in nineteen eighty, from Sanford Grossman and Joseph Stiglitz. If prices already reflected all information, no one would pay to gather any, and then prices couldn't reflect it. So a market can be informative only if it is never perfectly so. Exactly how far short it has to fall is still argued about.
