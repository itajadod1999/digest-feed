Now a minute of French, and then the European news in it.

How would you say you got taken for a ride? Not defrauded in any formal sense. Somebody got one over on you, and you walked straight into it.

Here it is.

Je me suis fait avoir.

English hands you a passive, so you go hunting for arnaquer or tromper and end up with something that sounds like a police report. French builds it with se faire plus an infinitive. I got myself had. And the verb is avoir, which here has nothing to do with having. It means to get someone.

And yesterday's, again. How would you say you can't be bothered?

J'ai la flemme.

And Tuesday's. How would you say something just doesn't appeal to you?

Ça me dit rien.

Back in English, and to Iran, where something real happened at the strait.

Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps said yesterday that Iran and Oman have agreed on each country's share of the waters of the Strait of Hormuz, and of the revenue from it. The two foreign ministries had issued a joint statement on Tuesday describing only an interim framework, with no final deal and no mention of fees. The Guard's account is further along than the diplomats', and it is the Guard's that moved oil.

Tehran is careful to add that a deal with Oman is not a reopening. Normalising traffic, it says, depends on Washington honouring a memorandum signed in June: lifting sanctions, ending the naval blockade of its ports, unfreezing its assets abroad. One story worth more time this morning.

Nvidia. Ninety-six billion dollars of revenue in a quarter, sixty billion of profit, and guidance for seventy percent growth next year. The shares fell one and a half percent during the session and recovered after hours.

Yesterday I said what would make this print matter was whether the customer base is widening, because the worry pressing on the stock all year has been circular financing: Nvidia arranging the money its customers buy its chips with, booking revenue now and credit risk later. The answer is partial. What Nvidia gave the market instead is a supply story. Demand is not the binding constraint, it says; capacity is. If that holds, the circularity argument weakens, because you don't finance a buyer already queueing.

The Journal's Asa Fitch points at the cost of that position. Nvidia is running something like a two hundred seventy-nine billion dollar supply-chain commitment, locking up memory, packaging and fabrication ahead of everyone else. That is how you win a supply war. It is also how a demand air pocket becomes an expensive inventory problem, because those commitments do not unwind when orders slow.

So the discount the stock carries to its own sector is not about this quarter's numbers. Nobody doubts the numbers. It is about whether a company growing at a hundred percent can also be the lender, the guarantor and the warehouse for its own market. Receivables and inventory, over the next two quarters, are where that gets answered.

The second story, and it is a settlement with an unusual shape.

Meta agreed to pay as much as eighteen billion dollars to end the states' case that it built Facebook and Instagram to keep children scrolling and knew what that did to them. The figures differ by outlet: Bloomberg and the Journal say eighteen billion across forty-seven or forty-eight states, the Economist says sixteen point seven. Meta admitted no wrongdoing and books about ten billion of legal expense this quarter.

The structure is the part worth having. Twelve point seven billion is fixed, paid to the states over ten years. The remaining five point three is contingent: Meta pays it only if TikTok and YouTube are also made to pay comparable penalties and adopt comparable safeguards. Meta has effectively told forty-seven attorneys general it will fund the rest of their war chest if they go and win the same fight twice more.

The product changes are dated and specific. Two hours a day for thirteen to seventeen year olds, no access between midnight and six, no notifications during school hours, likes hidden from minors.

Set against what was at stake, this is cheap. The states were seeking up to one point four trillion dollars, close to Meta's entire market value; eighteen billion is about a month of revenue. Florida's attorney general is not part of the deal and is suing separately. Whether any of it changes anything for teenagers is another question. The psychologists the Times asked called it a symbolic victory of uncertain effect.

One thing to sit with this morning.

Habit is thus the enormous fly-wheel of society, its most precious conservative agent. It alone is what keeps us all within the bounds of ordinance, and saves the children of fortune from the envious uprisings of the poor.

That is William James, from the chapter on habit in The Principles of Psychology, published in eighteen ninety after twelve years of work he had come to loathe. He described the finished book to his publisher as a loathsome, distended, tumefied, bloated, dropsical mass. He had trained as a physician, never practised, and wrote much of it depressed.

The observation I'd make is about the second sentence, which people quoting the first usually leave off. James is not describing habit as a personal mechanism there. He is describing it as a political one, and saying plainly that it is what keeps the fisherman at sea through the winter and the poor from coming for the rich. He offers that as the good news.
