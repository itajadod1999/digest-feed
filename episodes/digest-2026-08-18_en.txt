One story worth more time this morning, and it is the largest single financing commitment I have read this year.

Nvidia has agreed to guarantee up to a hundred five billion dollars of financing so that OpenAI can lease a data centre campus in Pike County, Ohio. The developer is SB Energy, owned by SoftBank. The site is designed for as much as eight gigawatts, with the first eight hundred megawatts live in twenty twenty-eight, on a twenty-year lease. Nvidia is also putting one and a half billion into SB Energy, and is the exclusive chip supplier.

Set that beside yesterday's item, Anthropic signing a nine-point-one-billion-dollar, twenty-year lease for a hundred ninety-one megawatts in Texas. Same structure, one order of magnitude apart.

Here is the mechanism, because guarantee is doing all the work in that sentence. Nvidia is not writing a cheque for a hundred five billion and is not buying the buildings. It is standing behind the obligations so lenders will fund the development against a tenant that has never turned a profit. The credit question stops being can OpenAI pay this rent for twenty years, and becomes will Nvidia cover it if they cannot.

The circularity is the part to hold onto. Nvidia guarantees the financing, the campus is contractually filled with Nvidia chips, and the revenue from those chips services the obligation Nvidia has guaranteed. Every leg is real. But the same balance sheet sits at both ends, and if demand for the output softens, it does not soften in one place.

What would make this concrete is disclosure. A guarantee of this size is a contingent liability, and how Nvidia characterises it in its next filing will tell you more than the announcement did.

The second story. The long end of the American bond market is repricing, and the short end is not.

More than a full percentage point separates the two-year from the thirty-year, and that gap keeps widening. The short end is about the policy rate, and at four point one eight the market expects no drama from the Federal Reserve soon. The long end is about two other things: where inflation settles over decades, and how much extra yield investors demand for holding a thirty-year promise. That second piece is the term premium, and it is what has moved.

Term premium rises when the supply of long bonds is heavy, when inflation has been unreliable, and when buyers doubt the fiscal path. All three apply. Inflation has run above target for five years, the Treasury is issuing a great deal of long-dated paper, and Saturday's Michigan survey had consumers expecting four point three percent inflation a year out.

Why it matters practically is that the long end is not a Federal Reserve instrument. The central bank sets the overnight rate. It does not set the thirty-year. So a committee that eases to support a weakening labour market can watch the long end go the other way, because easing into unanchored expectations is what makes a thirty-year bond less attractive.

Which is why tomorrow's minutes matter more than usual. Three officials dissented in favour of raising rates at the last meeting. If that was a serious argument rather than a gesture, the market has to price a committee that might tighten into weakness. Kevin Warsh gives his first keynote as chair at Jackson Hole at the end of the month.

What to watch is narrow: whether five point three one holds, and what the thirty-year does on a day with no headline attached to it.

One thing to sit with this morning.

You cannot speak of the ocean to a frog in a well, for he is bound to his little space. You cannot speak of ice to a summer insect, for it is bound to a single season.

That is from the Zhuangzi, from the chapter usually called The Floods of Autumn, assembled in China during the Warring States period, around the fourth century before the common era. The man the book is named for held a minor post overseeing a lacquer garden. The story that follows him is that the king of Chu sent officials to make him prime minister, and he refused, comparing the office to an ox fattened for years to be led out in embroidered cloth and sacrificed.

The observation I would make is that the image is usually quoted as a lesson in humility, and it does not read that way. It is a conversation-ender. It says the other party cannot follow the argument because of what they are, which is a claim no reply can touch. Whoever reaches for it has already decided which of the two is the frog.
