Good morning. Tuesday, August fourth. Here's what happened.

Starting with markets. Stocks opened the week strong. The Dow closed at a record, up one point three two percent. The S and P five hundred added one point four eight percent to seven thousand six hundred. The Nasdaq gained two point one three percent. Brent crude fell seven point four percent to eighty three dollars and forty nine cents on hopes for American and Iranian diplomacy. Amazon rose four point six percent and crossed three trillion dollars in market value for the first time.

The reason the rally has kicked into another gear is corporate profits. Eighty six percent of S and P five hundred companies that have reported this season have beaten analysts' earnings per share forecasts, according to FactSet. Palantir grew overall revenue ninety three percent and commercial revenue one hundred forty nine percent, and raised its full year guidance. Its chief executive, Alex Karp, called the sales growth otherworldly. The rally is also no longer only artificial intelligence. The equal weighted S and P five hundred, a broader measure of the index, is at a record high, and Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan Chase and Citigroup have all raised their year end targets. American factory activity hit a four year high in July.

Against that, borrowing costs keep climbing. The ten year Treasury note traded at four point six nine percent this morning, up from three point nine five percent on the eve of the Iran war in late February. The thirty year touched five point two eight percent. And the Federal Reserve is now more likely to raise than to cut. At its July twenty ninth meeting the committee held the federal funds rate in a range of three and a half to three and three quarters percent, on a nine to three vote. Lorie Logan of Dallas, Beth Hammack of Cleveland and Neel Kashkari of Minneapolis all dissented in favor of a quarter point increase. That is the most dissents at a single meeting since September of twenty sixteen.

Now the yen. Japan confirmed yesterday that the United States Treasury helped prop up the currency for the first time since nineteen ninety eight, buying yen alongside Tokyo's own intervention. The amount is not public, but Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent was photographed on Friday with a to do list containing a single item: buy five to ten billion dollars of yen. The yen dollar rate had hit a forty year low on concerns about Japanese government debt, low interest rates and rising oil import costs. The Nikkei fell two point two percent. The yen is slipping again today. The Wall Street Journal reports that a little used Federal Reserve backstop is being put to work bankrolling Japan's defense of its currency. Bessent says the United States will not hesitate to buy more.

One consequence worth watching is the carry trade, in which investors borrow cheap yen and reinvest in riskier assets. If both Tokyo and Washington now want a stronger yen, investors have to rethink that trade.

Moving to deals and credit. AstraZeneca and Bristol Myers Squibb are in talks on what would be one of the largest mergers in pharmaceutical history, according to the Financial Times. AstraZeneca is worth about two hundred forty two billion dollars, Bristol about one hundred thirty two billion. Together they would be the world's fourth largest drugmaker, generating roughly one hundred seven billion dollars in annual revenue. Neither company commented. Investors did not like it. AstraZeneca's London listed shares fell as much as nine percent. Analysts at Jefferies said they were, quote, a bit perplexed, given the strength of AstraZeneca's growth. Bristol loses exclusivity next year on two drugs that make up half its sales. One explanation offered is that AstraZeneca wants more American business, which already accounts for about half its revenue, and that a combined cancer portfolio would be the industry's largest.

The New York Times obtained OpenAI's capitalization table. Investors have committed more than one hundred eighty billion dollars to the company, which may go public as soon as early next year near a one trillion dollar valuation. Founders and employees with vested shares hold Class A common stock equal to forty eight point nine three percent of the company. Microsoft owns more than a quarter, having invested more than thirteen billion dollars since twenty nineteen. Earlier round investors hold nearly twenty percent. Investors in the March round, which raised one hundred twenty two billion dollars at an eight hundred fifty two billion dollar post money valuation, hold only about six percent so far, because the money arrives in installments. Amazon completed a thirty five billion dollar second installment last week. Sam Altman still holds no equity.

FIFA's plan to spin off its commercial assets and sell a twenty percent stake at a twenty billion dollar valuation has been abandoned, after boycott threats and calls for president Gianni Infantino to resign. JPMorgan's role, recruiting investors, was led by Mary Erdoes. Infantino worked on the plan with Josh Kushner. Lawyers for European soccer's governing body have now demanded that FIFA retain all documentation related to the project, including any correspondence with the bank. JPMorgan played the same role in the failed Super League in twenty twenty one, and issued an unusual apology for it.

SpaceX reports its first quarterly results as a public company after the close today. It priced its initial public offering at one hundred thirty five dollars a share on June eleventh, raising eighty five point seven billion dollars, the largest offering on record. The stock is down roughly half from its June sixteenth high. The company lost four point nine billion dollars in twenty twenty five, and a further four point two eight billion in the first quarter of this year on four point six nine billion in revenue. Its first share lockup expires Thursday.

Also, the Treasury Department fined U B S one hundred twenty five million dollars for inadequate anti money laundering controls, eight years after the Swiss bank was punished for the same problem. Now real estate. Commercial mortgage backed securities distress hit a twenty twenty six high in July. CRED iQ puts the overall distress rate, meaning every loan either specially serviced or thirty or more days delinquent, at ten point nine one percent. That is up from nine point nine seven percent in April, and it has risen three months running. The special servicing rate is ten point three eight percent, up forty two basis points, the sharpest single month move of the year. Delinquency is eight point six eight percent, up twenty four basis points. By property type, office is sixteen point six five percent, mixed use thirteen point zero one, multifamily eleven point two one, industrial two point three five, and self storage zero point two eight. The widening gap between the special servicing and delinquency rates suggests loans are being transferred to workout before payments are actually missed.

One example of what that eventually resolves into. The loan on Gateway Center in Pittsburgh liquidated at a sixty four point eight million dollar loss. The trust held about ninety two million dollars against an original ninety four million dollar loan on four office buildings totaling one and a half million square feet. Gross proceeds from the note sale were thirty seven point four million, against an appraisal of sixty nine point five million. Two tranches were written off entirely, and the Class E junior tranche lost half its value. The loan went to special servicing in September of twenty twenty four and matured that January, with occupancy as low as fifty five percent.

In Queens, LMXD and BedRock closed a two hundred fifty million dollar loan from New York Life Investment Management for a five hundred sixty unit mixed income building at thirty five ten Steinway Street in Astoria. The partners acquired the site for forty seven point seven million dollars. A quarter of the apartments are set aside as affordable at sixty percent of area median income. J L L arranged the financing. The site is part of what remained after the much larger Innovation Queens development was scaled back.

And JPMorgan Chase said yesterday it intends to deploy more than seven hundred fifty billion dollars through twenty thirty five under its American Dream Initiative. That is an increase of more than two hundred billion over its previous commitment, and roughly forty percent more housing capital than the bank deployed over the past decade. It says the money will finance one million affordable housing units and help five hundred thousand buyers purchase homes, beginning in five markets including Alabama. The effort is run by Karen Purcell, who took over community development banking seven months ago after sixteen years at Bank of America.

Over to France. Consumer prices rose two point one percent year on year in July, according to the provisional estimate from INSEE, up from one point eight percent in June. On the harmonized European measure it was two point four percent, after two percent. Month on month, prices rose zero point six percent, after falling zero point three percent in June. INSEE attributes the acceleration to services, particularly accommodation and communications, and to energy, with gas and petroleum products rising. Final figures come August fourteenth.

The Livret A savings rate rose to one point seven percent on August first, from one point five percent, and the sustainable development passbook moves with it. The Livret d'Épargne Populaire stays at two point five percent. Economy Minister Roland Lescure announced the rates on July fifteenth, on the recommendation of the governor of the Banque de France, Emmanuel Moulin.

And the Middle East. The United States and Iran cannot agree on whether they are negotiating. A day after President Trump said he called off a major strike because, in his words, the perimeters of a deal has been agreed to, Iran's foreign ministry said there are no talks with the United States, only discussions with Oman about managing the Strait of Hormuz. Trump called that claim unbelievably duplicitous on Truth Social, said the two countries are in fact talking, and later told reporters it was Iran's last chance to sign a deal. He also said Exxon and Chevron were making too much money from the war's disruptions.

Iranian officials say the Oman talks are in their final stages and would establish a new shipping route, different from the ones used before, and separate from the question of whether the strait itself reopens. Washington wants free international navigation. Tehran insists on keeping a role in managing passage. The strait, which carries about a fifth of world oil supply, has been largely blocked since the war began in late February. Even if it reopens, transit costs are expected to exceed prewar levels.

Israeli and Lebanese delegations met at the American embassy in Rome today for a new round of talks. Hezbollah said the negotiations bring nothing but shame, which drew criticism in Beirut. Qatar accused Israel of holding up the second phase of the twenty point Gaza plan, saying Hamas has met its obligations. That plan requires Hamas to disarm fully, which it has refused to do. And Yemen's Houthis said they struck Najran Airport in Saudi Arabia.
